The Brief

Who?The client

Tommy Hilfiger, an iconic American fashion brand known for its prep style and cultural collaborations. Tommy was introducing a new Monogram logo in its Andy Warhol inspired FACTORY Fall ’22 brand campaign but needed a way to communicate the new mark’s meaning and equity.

What?The campaign

Introduced the brand’s new TH Monogram collection through a social campaign featuring interviews with NYC’s biggest icons, artists, and tastemakers, exploring the people behind their personas and tying into the monogram’s symbolism of personal branding. The monogram, designed in collaboration with illustrator Fergus Purcell, reimagined classic prep motifs with a contemporary twist.

Where?The channels

Instagram & TikTok for interview content and campaign rollout, complemented by integration into the Fall 2022 NYFW runway show for broader exposure.

Why?Cultural impact

In a digital era where individuals curate personal brands, the campaign addressed the need to refresh Tommy Hilfiger’s heritage logo to resonate with modern audiences by emphasizing its representation of authenticity and self-expression.

The idea

Q & Andy

To create a storytelling device for Monogram we started by uncovering its meaning: a signature stamp used to identify a someone. We evolved this definition, leaning into the tension between person and public persona, through a social “Q & Andy” inspired series.

Set in our “famebox,” a physical manifestation of Warhol’s FACTORY confined to the 1x1 dimensions of social media, we asked talent questions that were inspired by Andy Warhol’s classic interviews.

The runway

Tommy Factory, live

The campaign had a live counterpart. On a rainy Sunday evening, Tommy Hilfiger made its big return to New York Fashion Week with a show inspired by Warhol’s Factory, staged at Brooklyn’s Skyline Drive-In in front of a star-studded front row. It was billed as a phygital runway experience.

The collection’s centerpiece was the monogram itself, an interlocking, geometric T and H designed with British illustrator and graphic designer Fergus Purcell. He dug through decades of the brand’s PVH archives to give East Coast prep a bolder, streetwear edge, in its core burgundy, navy and snow white.

Results

The campaign generated significant industry buzz, debuting the monogram at NYFW 2022 and earning coverage in major fashion outlets, contributing to the collection’s global rollout. The launch content garnered 4.3M total views, 8.6M in reach and featured marquee talent like Kate Moss, Travis Barker, Jon Batiste, Lady Bunny and many more.
