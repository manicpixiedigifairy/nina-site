Nike / Air Max Day

Air MaxDay 22

For the broader hardcore sneakerhead community, Air Max Day 2022 (which marked the 35th anniversary of the Air Max 1) shifted from abstract digital hubs into pure product storytelling, regional hype, and archival history. The 2022 approach transformed standard consumer feeds into a sprawling, multi-platform gallery.

01The moment

A milestone on the biggest day

Air Max Day is already one of the most anticipated dates on the sneaker calendar. In 2022, March 26 also marked 35 years of the Air Max 1, a milestone big enough to raise the stakes on a day fans were already counting down to.

02The idea

Turn hype into anticipation

Use 35 years of Air to turn excitement into anticipation. Instead of simply counting down, the content celebrated the people who made Air Max more than a shoe: their first pairs, their memories and the history behind the silhouette. Every piece fed the build to March 26 and kept fans looking for what dropped next.

03The rollout

Owned and always on

Short-form films cut for vertical viewing. Interactive Sliders and Add Yours stickers, Stories that prompted fans to share their own memories, quizzed hardcore sneakerheads on their “ancient texts” (aka Tinker Hatfield’s original sketches), and dropped fun Air facts and style tips featuring UGC.

My role

I created organic and reactive content that anchored the narrative of the entire Air Max Day ecosystem on Nike’s owned channels, fed fan enthusiasm, drove engagement, amplified reach and introduced casual sneakerheads to proper Air Max Day etiquette.

On Air, the designer

Juliana Sagat

A footwear designer from Paris, raised in Saint-Ouen and trained at Studio Bercot, who came to Nike in Portland after Marc Jacobs, Kenzo, Givenchy and Isabel Marant.

For the Air Max 1’s 35th, she spoke to Nike’s design shift toward women, community-driven insight and tactile, exaggerated tooling, like the double-stacked Air of the Air Max Furyosa.

Your City

UGC on @nikesportswear

“Show Us Your Air” turned Nike’s feeds into a multi-platform gallery. On March 26, community content tags, curated with local boutiques and Nike partners, invited fans to post their collections, unboxings and on-foot grids. Their looks were repurposed into city-tagged posts that sent people to shop the collection on Instagram.
