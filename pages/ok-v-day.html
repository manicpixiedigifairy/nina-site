The Brief

Keep it just OK

01 · The Ask♥

Past the holidays

Extend AT&T’s “Just OK” platform past peak holiday season, into a Q1 audience worn out on spending but still hunting for a deal, in an original, attention-grabbing way.

Q1 / Post-holidayValue-seekingBudget-conscious

  1. Spending fatigueAfter the holidays, wallets are tired and wary of one more ask.
  2. Deal-seeking intentTired wallets still hunt for value, so something free earns a look.
  3. A budget-conscious mindsetEvery dollar gets weighed, and “just OK” isn’t worth paying for.
02 · The Idea♥

Gorgeous cards, lukewarm feelings

A series of “just OK” Valentine’s cards with all the lush, hand-lettered trimmings of the real thing and none of the commitment.

Anti-aspirationalDeadpanDeeply affectionate, in its own cynical way

  1. Given away freeAt a Los Angeles pop-up.
  2. Caught on cameraWith a hidden camera rolling.
  3. Every card flipped to the pointNetworks, like Valentines, should never be just OK.

The Flip Side

Every card reliably delivered the message:

Networks, like Valentines, should never be just OK.

The cards were the joke. The network was the point: you can laugh off a mediocre Valentine, but nobody laughs off mediocre coverage.

Results

How it did (better than OK)

By celebrating comedic, subpar milestones, the campaign cut through one of the most cluttered advertising holidays of the year with a distinct, memorable voice, and landed the core brand message: like a card for someone you love, your network coverage shouldn’t be just OK.
